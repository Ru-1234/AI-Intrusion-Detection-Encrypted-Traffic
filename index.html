01
The problem

The payload is locked. The metadata isn't.

TLS, QUIC and DNS-over-HTTPS hide what's inside every packet, so old-school payload inspection goes blind. But a detector can still see how big a flow is, which way it's going and how it's timed. That's all we use. We never decrypt anything.

02
What we read

13 recent papers, and none of them measure cost the same way

Phase 1 covered three Q1 papers (Adaptive Feature Selection, FIRE-G, MTDecipher). Phase 2 added ten more from 2025–2026. Accuracy looked great everywhere. Speed was a different story.

Detection latency as each paper reports it, on a log scale. Hardware, datasets and what's being timed all differ, so these can't be compared fairly. That's gap G2. DC-GL only reports time per epoch (208 s for detection), so it has no bar.

04
Our procedure

Twelve steps, from raw flows to explained alerts

Every step ran in one notebook, top to bottom, with five random seeds.

    05
    The data

    19 million flows in, 241 thousand clean ones out

    We used two public datasets, CIC-IDS-2017 and CSE-CIC-IDS2018, and kept only flows on encrypted ports (22, 443, 465, 993, 995, 8443).

    Bar length is on a log scale.

    470,510duplicate flows removed
    23,262flows with conflicting labels removed
    39%of 2018 infiltration flows were identical to benign ones

    After cleaning we found 0 identical rows across train, validation and test, so there is no leakage. Final families: benign, SSH (both datasets), PortScan (2017 only) and Infiltration (2018 only).

    06
    Known vs unseen

    Great on attacks it knows. Blind to ones it doesn't.

    Every threshold is set so only 1% of normal flows raise an alert. Splits are by time, never random.

    Mean over 5 seeds. Left and middle bars: higher is better. Dashed line on AUROC: 0.5 is a coin flip. Right bar: lower is better, log scale. "Caught" means attack flows flagged at the 1% false-alarm threshold. Isolation Forest and Elliptic Envelope learn only from benign flows.

    Protocol B: an attack family hidden during training

    G1 not achieved

    No method caught an unseen family at 1% false alarms: not our detector, not the tree models, not the benign-only ones. An AUROC below 0.5 on SSH means unseen SSH looks more normal than real normal traffic. Flow statistics alone are not enough, and no ablation changed this.

    08
    CPU cost

    0.38 ms per flow on one CPU thread

    One measuring rule for every model: time per flow including preprocessing, over 1,000 flows after warm-up, on a single laptop CPU thread.

    0.38 msp50 latency
    p95 0.49 ms

    0.03 MB

    Top left is best: fast and accurate. Isolation Forest and Elliptic Envelope are fast or slow but cannot detect known attacks. A network 5× smaller than ours (about 1,400 parameters) scores the same AUROC, 0.920, against 0.926 for a large one.

    G2 achieved

    Same measurement for every method. Honest limit: this is a laptop CPU, not an edge device. Our slower v3 version (quantile preprocessing) costs 10.7 ms, so stability on a new network is bought with speed.

    09
    A hidden effect

    Why published accuracy looks so high

    Many papers report 94–99% accuracy with random splits. We re-ran the same Random Forest on the same data under three setups.

    13.5%of test flows also appear in training when duplicates are kept
    (SSH 47.9%, PortScan 68.5%)
    5–17%false alarms on test days with a time split, against a 1% target

    Random Forest accuracy: time split 85.2%, random split with duplicates 90.0%, random split cleaned 95.9%. DC-GL's published 97.3% is from its paper, not re-run by us.

    What this means

    Most of the gap between our numbers and published ones comes from how the model is tested, not from the model. Published accuracy on CICIDS-style data depends strongly on the split.

    10
    Explanations

    Why did it flag that flow?

    We first used SHAP on a copy of the detector, but it disagreed with the real detector, so we threw it out and explained the detector itself with Integrated Gradients and later Expected Gradients.

    0.11top-10 overlap of the SHAP copy with KernelSHAP. Rejected.
    2.5–3.6×score drops faster than random when top features are deleted
    ~2%completeness error
    1.47 msper alert, vs 34.8 ms for KernelSHAP
    • Stable, once grouped68 features form only 42 correlation clusters. Seed-to-seed overlap is 0.79 (known) and 0.73 (unseen) at cluster level, and up to 0.855 with Expected Gradients.
    • Explains our G3 failureThe MLP relies on the feature clusters that shift most between datasets. The Random Forest relies on steadier ones.
    • Top features for unseen InfiltrationTCP header and window features: fwd_header_len, min_seg_size_fwd, init_win_fwd.
    G5 partly achieved · best 5 of 6

    Explanations are faithful, complete, cheap and stable. The weak spot: since G1 failed, there are few unseen alerts to explain (103 in v1, none in v3).

    11
    Taking parts away

    What mattered, and what didn't

    • Flag features matter mostDropping them cuts AUROC from 0.920 to 0.832. Timing 0.899, volume 0.903, length 0.912.
    • A tiny network is enoughAbout 1,400 parameters gets 0.920, the large one (~61,000) gets 0.926.
    • The port is not a shortcutAdding it changes AUROC only from 0.920 to 0.923.
    • Feature selection hurtTop-10 features 0.862, top-20 0.885, top-40 0.904.
    • No part fixes unseen attacksEvery Protocol B variant stays at AUROC 0.34–0.52.
    12
    The honest bit

    What changed from the plan, and what we can't claim

    • Phase 2: first 10 packets with a 1D-CNN or GRUPhase 3: flow statistics with an MLP. The CSVs have no packets.
    • Phase 2: five datasetsPhase 3: two. The others had no usable attack labels.
    • Phase 2: TLS-filtered flowsPhase 3: an encrypted-port filter. The CSVs have no TLS fields.
    • Phase 2: drift windows (G4)Phase 3: dropped for scope, so G4 is not claimed.
    • Phase 2: edge-device latencyPhase 3: laptop CPU, one thread.

    Limits

    1. Port 443 may carry non-TLS traffic.
    2. Attack family and dataset are partly mixed: PortScan only in 2017, Infiltration only in 2018.
    3. 2017 has no timestamps, so time order is file and row order.
    4. Infiltration labels are noisy: best AUROC is only 0.775.
    5. Thresholds drift: 1% on validation becomes 5.5–16.8% on 2018 test days.
    6. Later versions were designed after seeing earlier results on the same data.
    Our takeaway

    Known attacks are detectable at low cost. Unseen families are not, at least not from flow statistics. We report this negative result, with the leakage findings, as a contribution.
